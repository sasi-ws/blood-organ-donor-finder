Donation Awareness

Learn about blood donation, organ donation, platelet donation, and common donation myths.

1. Blood Donation

Donated blood can help patients who need transfusions because of accidents, surgery, certain illnesses, or other medical conditions.

Who can donate?

Eligibility depends on age, health, weight, medical history, and local blood donation rules. A qualified blood bank professional must confirm whether someone can donate.

How does it work?

  1. Register with an authorised blood donation service.
  2. Answer the health screening questions.
  3. Have your eligibility checked by trained staff.
  4. If eligible, follow the staff's instructions before and after donation.

2. Organ Donation

Organ and tissue donation may help people who need transplantation. Donation procedures and eligibility depend on medical assessment and applicable laws.

How can someone express their wishes?

  • Learn about the official donation process.
  • Discuss your wishes with your family.
  • Follow the registration or consent process recognised by the relevant authorities.
  • Ask a qualified healthcare professional if you have questions about eligibility.
Recording a pledge on this website expresses an intention. It does not itself confirm medical eligibility or guarantee that donation will take place.

3. Myths and Facts

Myth: Anyone can donate blood at any time.

Fact: Donors must meet the applicable eligibility requirements and pass screening.

Myth: Blood donation has no screening process.

Fact: Authorised donation services screen donors and handle donated blood according to safety procedures.

Myth: An online organ pledge guarantees donation.

Fact: A pledge records a person's intention. Actual donation depends on medical, consent, and legal requirements.

Myth: Only one type of blood donation exists.

Fact: Depending on the service, people may donate whole blood or specific components, such as platelets, when eligible.

4. Platelets and Dengue

Platelets help blood clot. Some patients with severe dengue develop dangerously low platelet counts or bleeding complications and need careful medical monitoring.

  • Only donate platelets through an authorised donation service.
  • Donation staff will assess your eligibility.
  • Not every dengue patient needs a platelet transfusion; treatment decisions belong to the medical team.
  • Follow official local health advice during dengue outbreaks.
Remember: This page provides general educational information, not medical advice. Contact an authorised blood bank or healthcare professional for current eligibility rules and donation guidance.
